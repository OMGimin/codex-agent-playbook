# Content Production

Infer purpose, readers, format, tone, language, and approximate depth from the request and existing samples. Ask only for consequential missing requirements under AGENTS.md. Use an available voice or artifact-format skill when it materially fits; do not force a document workflow on a short answer.

## Draft from evidence

For news, technical claims, or other source-dependent material, use `source-backed-research` as needed. If evidence is already supplied and sufficient, reuse it with its date and limitations. User-provided text is not automatically verified fact. Preserve the user's opinions as opinions; do not invent personal experience, quotes, credentials, or completed tests.

Choose an outline that serves the reader's question. Lead with the main point, develop evidence and implications, and make uncertainty understandable. Avoid turning tentative reports into definitive headlines. Keep citations attached to the claims they support; synthesized conclusions must be identifiable as analysis.

## Review and deliver

Check factual support, dates, names, statistics, links, internal consistency, readability, and fit with the brief. For a substantial or consequential article, an independent fact-checking pass may help; a short low-risk edit needs no mandatory second writer.

Keep writing quality and factual correctness as separate checks. Correct unsupported claims or clearly qualify them. Do not fill gaps with invented examples presented as real events. Reuse valid research unless the time-sensitive claim requires refreshing.

Deliver the requested usable draft or artifact, with unresolved publication-critical gaps visible. A draft may be complete even when publication was not requested. Uploading, posting, scheduling, emailing, or changing a CMS requires matching authorization; internal editorial approval does not supply it. If publication was explicitly authorized, honor that scope and any platform-specific safeguards without redundant confirmation.
